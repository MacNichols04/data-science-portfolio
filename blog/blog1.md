What is Data Science to Me?

# Learning Data Science in a Social World

Data science interests me because numbers can be used to explain things that happen in the real world. I first became interested in statistics through sports. I always liked looking at player stats, comparing performances, and trying to understand why certain teams or players were more successful than others. As I have learned more about data science, I have realized that the same ideas can be applied to many areas outside of sports.

Learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts is important because data is often connected to real people. A dataset may look like rows and columns, but each observation can represent a person, decision, behavior, or real-life experience. Because of this, data scientists have a responsibility to think carefully about how data is collected, analyzed, and interpreted.

One responsibility is avoiding conclusions that the data cannot actually support. A pattern or correlation does not always mean that one thing causes another. It is also important to consider bias. Some groups may be underrepresented in a dataset, while others may have more information collected about them. Missing data, measurement choices, and the way variables are defined can all influence the conclusions that come from an analysis.

Another responsibility is communicating results clearly. Data can easily be presented in a misleading way if visualizations, averages, or statistics are taken out of context. I think data scientists should explain both what their results show and what they do not show.

Through this course, I hope to improve my ability to collect, clean, visualize, and interpret data using Python. I am especially interested in sports analytics, but I also want to understand how data science can be applied to business and other industries. My goal is to become better at finding meaningful patterns in data while also understanding the limitations and responsibilities that come with using it.
